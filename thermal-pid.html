Thermal System Modeling & PID Control

Technical analysis, original simulation results and engineering decisions.

ESIEE Paris · Project team: Rania Chettar · Anaïs Sallard

An experimental oven was represented by a fourth-order transfer function, and temperature regulation was investigated using feedback and a PID controller in Cadence/PSpice. The model used an electrical voltage as the analogue of the temperature setpoint and response.

Plant and closed-loop model

Thermal plant transfer function
\(G(s)=\frac{10}{(1+0.1s)(1+s)^2(1+10s)}\)
\(T(s)=\frac{C(s)G(s)}{1+C(s)G(s)}\)

With the original feedback configuration, the recorded 1 V step response reached approximately 1.46 V and settled near 0.9 V. This corresponds to 46% overshoot relative to the command and a 10% steady-state tracking error.

PID controller and Ziegler–Nichols tuning

Parallel PID transfer function
\(C(s)=K_{\mathrm{p}}+\frac{K_{\mathrm{i}}}{s}+K_{\mathrm{d}}s\)

The proportional-only experiment found sustained oscillation around an ultimate gain of 2, with an oscillation period of approximately 6.33 s. Applying the classical Ziegler–Nichols closed-loop rules gave the reported initial gains Kp = 1.2, Ki ≈ 0.38, Kd ≈ 0.95. This removed the steady-state error but increased the overshoot in the simulated step response.

Refining the transient response

A parameter sweep holding proportional gain at 1.2 identified an improved reported configuration: Ki = 0.20, Kd = 3. The resulting response showed approximately 0.15 V overshoot and a 5% settling time of 4.5 s, compared with the initial PID response of roughly 0.6 V overshoot and 11 s settling time.

The study also explored an electronic PID realization. The results are simulation-based and do not establish performance on a physical oven under sensor noise, actuator saturation or thermal disturbances.
