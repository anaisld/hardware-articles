Embedded Control · Engineering Case Study

NI myRIO Motor Control & LabVIEW

Technical analysis, original simulation results and engineering decisions.

ESIEE Paris · Project team: Rania Chettar · Anaïs Sallard

A professional food mixer served as a case study for integrating a DC motor, an LCD and sensors with an NI myRIO controller. The LabVIEW virtual instrument (VI) was organized around a persistent execution loop and case structures, allowing the operator to change recipes without restarting the application.

Program selection and PWM actuation

Four operating modes were assigned different speed commands: dough, cream, stuffing and eggs. The reference maximum speed was 5 revolutions per second. The chosen command was normalized to drive a PWM duty-cycle input between zero and one.

Duty-cycle command
\(D = \frac{n_{\mathrm{set}}}{n_{\mathrm{max}}}, \quad 0\le D\le1\)

This relation is a command mapping, not a guarantee that actual shaft speed is proportional to duty cycle. Load torque, friction and supply voltage can change the measured speed.

Encoder feedback

Motor speed was estimated from the change in encoder count over a sampling interval, taking account of 12 pulses per motor revolution and the reported gearbox reduction ratio of 19.225.

Encoder-based output-shaft speed
\(n_{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{\Delta N}{12\times19.225\times\Delta t}\)

The LCD was initialized through I²C and updated with date, time and selected program information. Rotation-mode selection was implemented in the VI.

What remained open

The original report notes that vibration detection using the accelerometer was not completed. It also documents an LCD refresh issue after stopping and restarting. Although the objective was speed regulation, the report does not demonstrate a validated closed-loop speed controller; it documents PWM control and encoder measurement.
