Interactive Paragliding Simulator: Sensors to Unity

Technical analysis, original simulation results and engineering decisions.

ESIEE Paris · Project team: Erwin Dumontel · Thomas Mateos · Anaïs Sallard · Loïc Bazonnais

This year-long engineering project transformed an existing paragliding training rig into an interactive simulator. The team combined mechanical prototyping, sensor electronics, STM32 firmware, Bluetooth communication and a Unity simulation environment.

Measuring pilot inputs

Two rotary encoders measured the brake-line movements, while two strain-gauge load cells estimated left–right weight transfer in the harness. The load cells were interfaced through HX711 amplifier/ADC modules. Their differential bridge output follows the Wheatstone-bridge principle and requires calibration before conversion into useful force estimates.

Weight-shift indicator, conceptually
\(\eta=\frac{F_{\mathrm{right}}-F_{\mathrm{left}}}{F_{\mathrm{right}}+F_{\mathrm{left}}}\)

This normalized relation explains how differential loading can represent a left–right command; it is a general interpretation, not a claim that the firmware implemented this exact expression.

Embedded acquisition and communication

An STM32 NUCLEO-F411RE acquired encoder counts and HX711 readings, combined the sensor inputs into a command value and transmitted it via UART to an HC-05 Bluetooth module. A separate serial channel supported debugging.

Mechanical prototyping

Missing encoder components were reproduced in CATIA and manufactured using 3D printing. The work included a replacement encoder wheel and custom sensor mounting brackets, followed by physical assembly and integration.

Interactive simulation

Unity received the transmitted controls and used them in several training scenarios, including precision landing and finding rising air currents. The project illustrates the complete path from physical interaction to sensor data, embedded processing, wireless transport and an interactive digital environment.
