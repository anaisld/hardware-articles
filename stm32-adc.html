STM32F4 Data Acquisition: Polling, Interrupts & Timer Triggers

Technical analysis, original simulation results and engineering decisions.

ESIEE Paris · Project team: Kévin Kao · Rania Chettar · Anaïs Sallard

This laboratory compared three acquisition strategies for an STM32F4 microcontroller: polling, interrupt-driven continuous conversion, and timer-triggered conversion. A potentiometer provided an analog input to ADC3 channel 5, while USART1 sent diagnostic data to a serial terminal.

ADC conversion timing

The ADC clock was derived from a 90 MHz peripheral clock with a divider of six. For a 12-bit STM32F4 ADC, the conversion time includes the selected sampling cycles plus approximately 12 conversion cycles.

ADC clock and approximate maximum conversion rate
\(f_{\mathrm{ADC}}=\frac{f_{\mathrm{PCLK2}}}{P}=15\,\mathrm{MHz}\)
\(f_{\mathrm{conv}}\approx\frac{f_{\mathrm{ADC}}}{N_{\mathrm{sample}}+12}\)

At 480 sampling cycles, this predicts about 30.5 kconversions/s. The measured polling result was around 30 kHz, consistent with the estimate within experimental resolution and software overhead.

Polling versus interrupts

Polling waits explicitly for the end-of-conversion flag, occupying processor time. Interrupt mode instead invokes a callback when a result is ready, allowing the main loop to perform other work. The team inspected values ranging across the 12-bit range, 0–4095, and transmitted readings over UART at 115200 bit/s.

Timer-driven sampling

TIM2 was then used to schedule conversions at a much lower, controlled rate. For a timer clock of 90 MHz, the update frequency follows:

Timer update frequency
\(f_{\mathrm{update}}=\frac{f_{\mathrm{TIM}}}{(\mathrm{PSC}+1)(\mathrm{ARR}+1)}\)

An initial configuration yielded 2 Hz instead of the intended 1 Hz; the report records the correction and an LED transition every second. This illustrates the importance of distinguishing timer update frequency from the toggling frequency of an indicator output.
