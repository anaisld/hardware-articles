Finding the Sweet Spot: Modeling Solar Power with a Boost Converter

From photovoltaic I–V curves to impedance matching and analog maximum-power-point tracking.

Based on an ESIEE Paris engineering laboratory report by Anaïs Sallard and Erwin Dumontel

A solar panel does not deliver a fixed amount of power. Its operating point depends on irradiance and on the electrical load. In this Cadence/PSpice study, we characterized a 32-cell photovoltaic model, evaluated a Boost DC–DC converter and explored an analog maximum-power-point-tracking (MPPT) architecture.

Finding the maximum-power point

The panel was simulated at two irradiance levels. The current remains nearly constant over much of the voltage range before dropping sharply near the open-circuit region. The maximum-power point (MPP) lies near the knee of the I–V curve.

Electrical power — DC power relationship
P = V × I

At 1000 W/m², the report estimates VMPP ≈ 24 V and IMPP ≈ 4.7 A, giving PMPP ≈ 112.8 W. At 300 W/m², VMPP ≈ 24 V and IMPP ≈ 1.4 A, giving PMPP ≈ 33.6 W. These values are approximate readings of the simulated characteristics.

Key observationReducing irradiance from 1000 to 300 W/m² lowers the available peak power from approximately 113 W to 34 W. The optimal voltage changes relatively little in these particular simulations, while the optimal current drops substantially.

Matching the panel to a Boost converter

A Boost converter uses an inductor, MOSFET switch, diode and output capacitor to raise the DC voltage. Adjusting the PWM duty cycle changes the effective input resistance seen by the photovoltaic panel, allowing the operating point to move toward the MPP.

Ohm’s law — resistance at the MPP
RMPP = VMPP / IMPP

The estimated optimal input resistances are 24/4.7 ≈ 5.1 Ω at 1000 W/m² and 24/1.4 ≈ 17.1 Ω at 300 W/m².

Ideal Boost converter — impedance transformation
Rin = Rload(1 − D)² ⇒ D = 1 − √(RMPP / Rload)

For a 50 Ω load, this ideal continuous-conduction model predicts D ≈ 1 − √(5.1/50) ≈ 0.68 and D ≈ 1 − √(17.1/50) ≈ 0.42. These estimates neglect conversion losses and conduction-mode effects.

PWM duty cycle — definition
D = ton / T

With a 20 µs switching period, the corresponding on-times are approximately 13.6 µs and 8.4 µs. The fixed-duty-cycle simulations below illustrate how power evolves under the two irradiance conditions; they are not proof of closed-loop MPPT operation.

Toward fully analog MPPT

The final circuit introduces an analog feedback concept: measure panel voltage and current, form a power-related signal, compare its evolution and adjust the Boost switching command. Filtering and integration time constants affect the trade-off between noise sensitivity, stability and tracking speed.

Validation boundaryThe analog MPPT schematic was studied, but its closed-loop response to irradiance steps or ramps was not simulated in the original lab. Tracking accuracy, settling time and efficiency therefore remain unverified.

Engineering takeaway

This study connects semiconductor source characteristics with power electronics and analog control. It demonstrates how an electrical model can identify an optimal operating point, how a switching converter can adapt the load seen by the source, and why a tracking loop must ultimately be assessed through dynamic simulation and measurement.
