One Pair of Wires, Two Directions of Data: Modeling a PSI5 Sensor Interface

A transistor-level exploration of how automotive sensors share power and information with an electronic control unit — validated through Cadence/PSpice simulations.

Automotive sensor networks must balance wiring complexity, power delivery and dependable signal detection. PSI5 addresses this challenge with a two-wire interface: an electronic control unit (ECU) powers connected sensors while exchanging information through distinct electrical signaling mechanisms.

Engineering objective
Build and connect ECU-side and sensor-side analog models, predict their operating levels, and compare the expected behavior with transient simulations in both communication directions.

ECU → Sensor: information carried by voltage

The ECU switches a transistor network to modulate the line voltage. Using the voltage-divider rule and a first-order base–emitter voltage approximation, the two states can be estimated before simulation.

Voltage-divider rule · ECU line levels
VB = VDD × Rlower / (Rupper + Rlower)
Vline ≈ VB − VBE

For the high state: VB ≈ 11.1 V, hence Vline ≈ 11.1 − 0.6 = 10.5 V. Switching the resistor network gives a low-state estimate of 7 V.

These are approximate operating levels of the simulated circuit, not universal PSI5 limits.

At the receiving end, the sensor uses an analog comparator stage to detect these transitions.

Sensor → ECU: information carried by current

In the opposite direction, the sensor varies the current drawn from the line. A transistor and emitter resistor establish the modulation current; the ECU senses the resulting change and reconstructs a logic-level response.

Ohm’s law · Sensor modulation current
I = V / R
IC ≈ (Vsync − VBE) / RE

With Vsync = 5 V, VBE ≈ 0.6 V and RE = 180 Ω:

ΔI ≈ (5 − 0.6) / 180 = 24.4 mA
First-order estimate for the switched current, assuming active-region transistor operation.

The sensor also includes a Zener-referenced supply and an RC/comparator stage. From the RC time-constant relationship, τ = R × C, the nominal estimate is τ = 100 kΩ × 3.3 nF = 330 µs. This characterizes the transient response, not necessarily the exact comparator pulse width.

What the simulations show

Downlink. The simulated line switches between the expected voltage levels, and the sensor’s receiving circuit responds to the changes.

Uplink. The sensor’s modulated current produces a corresponding response at the ECU receiver, with analog transition delays visible in the simulated waveforms.

Combined operation. The final test drives both signaling paths together on the shared connection. Their corresponding receiver outputs remain observable under the simulated conditions.

Engineering takeaway

The key result: circuit calculations and transient simulations support the operation of the modeled analog PSI5 interface in both directions. The exercise connects transistor biasing, current sensing, comparator behavior, RC dynamics and hierarchical circuit design into one practical sensor-link study.

Validation scope: this is a functional analog simulation, not a certification of PSI5 protocol compliance, electromagnetic compatibility or worst-case hardware robustness.
