Analog Signal Processing · Engineering Case Study

Pulsed Laser Detection Under Ambient-Light Interference

Technical analysis, original simulation results and engineering decisions.

ESIEE Paris · Project team: Erwin Dumontel · Anaïs Sallard

The goal was to detect short laser pulses using a phototransistor despite strong ambient illumination and a 100 Hz component from artificial lighting. The sensor was represented by a current source combining background illumination, periodic interference and the useful laser pulses.

Separating useful pulses from interference

Because the laser pulse is brief while ambient illumination changes relatively slowly, frequency-selective processing can suppress low-frequency components. In a first-order high-pass approximation:

First-order high-pass response
\(H_{\mathrm{HP}}(s)=\frac{sRC}{1+sRC},\quad f_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{1}{2\pi RC}\)

The original architecture combined parallel high-pass and low-pass paths through a summing stage to attenuate unwanted background components. The effectiveness depends on their relative gains and signs, not merely on placing filters in parallel.

Hysteresis and logic-level shaping

A comparator with hysteresis helps prevent repeated switching when noise causes a signal to hover near a threshold. The report describes a following level-shifting stage intended to produce a 0–5 V logic output.

Hysteresis window
\(\Delta V_{\mathrm{H}}=V_{\mathrm{TH+}}-V_{\mathrm{TH-}}\)

Simulations compared the baseline circuit with variants using feedback and a bipolar transistor to adjust the phototransistor operating point. These alternatives reduced the influence of slow background fluctuations in the reported waveforms.

Low-signal behavior and limitations

The report also studied weak laser pulses. The analysis relied on idealized amplifier models and PSpice waveforms; it did not include measured detection probability or false-alarm rates. The circuit is therefore best described as a simulated analog detection chain, not a field-qualified optical detector.
